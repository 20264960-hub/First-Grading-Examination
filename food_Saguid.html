German Schnitzel (Schweineschnitzel)

By Kimberly Killebrew

A schnitzel is a highly popular, classic European dish consisting of a boneless cutlet of meat that is pounded until very thin, coated in breading, and pan-fried until golden brown and intensely crispy. Derived from the German word schnitt (meaning "to cut"), the dish relies on a straightforward, three-step breading technique: dredging the meat in flour, dipping it in beaten egg wash, and coating it with fine breadcrumbs.

Ingredients

  • 4 boneless pork steaks or chops
  • salt and freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/2 cup all-purpose flour combined with 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 large eggs, lightly beaten
  • 3/4 cup plain breadcrumbs
  • Oil for frying (use a neutral-tasting oil with a high smoke point)

Procedures

  1. Pound the meat very thin, no more than 1/4 inch thick
  2. Next dip the pork into the egg mixture, coating all sides
  3. Then coat the pork with the breadcrumbs
  4. Don’t press the breadcrumbs into the meat
  5. Just softly coat the pork on both sides and all edges, and then gently shake off any excess
  6. Fry the Schnitzel immediately
  7. Make sure the oil is hot enough but not too hot
  8. Remove the Schnitzel from the fry pan and place them briefly on a plate lined with paper towels
  9. Transfer them to serving plates and garnish with slices of lemon and fresh parsley sprigs
  10. Enjoy!
